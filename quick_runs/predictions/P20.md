# P20 a frontier, different-family judge: does the instruction-agreement behaviour generalise?

Written 2026-11-17, after the five-pass review loop and before any P20 run. the author supplied $100 of Anthropic API
credits and the key (kept in `~/.config/reveries/anthropic.env`, outside the repo) and asked to use a Claude model as
the guide while the agent stays Gemma 4 31B. Code: the `ClaudeLLM` backend in `reveries/llm.py` and the G3c / G3f
arms in `quick_runs/p13/guide.py`, already committed. Every call goes through a hard USD cap and a per-call ledger.

## Why

Every review round rated the missing frontier or closed-model guide as the one high-severity omission: the mechanism
(an instruction-tuned judge ranks the instructions above a lesson's claim) rests on two open ~30B models (Gemma 4 31B
and Qwen3.8-27B). A guide that is stronger and from a third family tests whether the behaviour is a property of
instruction-tuned judges in general or of small open models.

140 Claude guide runs (about 300 API calls at R = 3). At list prices and ~6.6k input / 3.3k output tokens per run,
Opus 5.5 is about $6-9 and Fable 5.1 about $16-23; the cap is set at $60 and the run stops if it is reached. The

Q3 (Qwen, 0.35), E-p / G3-p (poisoned), E-pc / G3-pc (agreeing).

Fix lessons, keep-`column_x` lessons, wrong lessons, shown, pooled: as in P14-P16. The Claude guide's sampling is the
model default (adaptive thinking, effort high); it is not forced to the agent's temperature, because it is a
different model with its own recommended settings (as the Qwen arm was).

## Expected direction

The central hypothesis is that the behaviour generalises: a stronger, different-family judge still ranks the
instructions above a lesson's claim when it has no data to check the claim.

1. **The fixes are still mostly rejected.** G3c and G3f each show a fix lesson on TF03 + TF05 in at most 3 of 10
   runs (Gemma G3: 1 of 10; Qwen Q3: 0 of 10). If a model shows them in more than half, that model is an exception,
   and the paper will say so.
2. **The reasons still cite the instructions.** At least half of each model's rejections of fix lessons cite the
   task's instructions (our pre-set pattern; Gemma 83%, Qwen 64%).
3. **Contradicting wrong lessons are blocked at least as well as Gemma.** G3c-p and G3f-p each show a P13 wrong
   lesson in at most 10 of 20 runs (G3-p: 5 of 20).
4. **Agreeing wrong lessons still pass.** With no data to contradict them, G3c-pc and G3f-pc each show an agreeing
   wrong lesson in at least 12 of 20 runs (G3-pc: 20 of 20). A stronger judge may catch more, so the threshold is
   lower than Gemma's.
5. **Usefulness is not added.** G3c and G3f pooled targeted checks are each within 0.15 of Gemma's 0.50, i.e. in
   [0.35, 0.65].
6. **No harm.** TF04 and TF06 within 0.01 of A0 in G3c and G3f.

## What each outcome means for the paper

- **1-2 hold for both models:** the instruction-agreement behaviour is not specific to small open models; it holds
  for a frontier judge from a third family. This is the generality the reviews asked for, and it strengthens the
  main claim.
- **1 fails for a model (it accepts the fixes):** that model is better at telling a correct lesson from a misleading
  one without data. The paper reports it as a limit on the generality claim, and as evidence that capability can
  help even without grounding.
- **4 holds:** the agreeing-wrong-lesson result, the sharpest in the paper, holds against a frontier judge: wrong
  advice in the task's own terms passes a stronger gate too.

## Limits stated in advance

- 5 runs per cell; the Claude guide uses its own default sampling, so it differs from Gemma in more than capability.
- No data profile is given to the Claude guide here (original prompt), so this tests the instruction-ranking
  behaviour, not whether a frontier judge uses data better.
- The spend is capped; if the cap is reached the run stops and the report says which arms completed.
